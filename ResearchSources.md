# Introduction sources
1.  GeeksforGeeks. (n.d.). Software testing: Fuzz testing. https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/software-engineering/software-testing-fuzz-testing/ Used for: introduction to fuzzers (slides 3 to 5).
2.  OWASP Foundation. (n.d.). Fuzzing. OWASP Community. https://community.owasp.org/Fuzzing Used for: advantages and disadvantages of fuzz testing (slides 13 and 14).
3.  Li, J., Zhao, B., & Zhang, C. (2018). Fuzzing: a survey. Cybersecurity, 1, Article 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42400-018-0002-y Used for: types of fuzzers (slides 6 and 9).
# Research sources
4.  Miller, B. P., Fredriksen, L., & So, B. (1990). An empirical study of the reliability of UNIX utilities. Communications of the ACM, 33(12), 32-44. Used for: the history of fuzzing and the 1988 origin of the term (slide 4).
5.  Fioraldi, A., Maier, D., Eissfeldt, H., & Heuse, M. (2020). AFL++: Combining incremental steps of fuzzing research. 14th USENIX Workshop on Offensive Technologies (WOOT 20). Used for: the tools slide and how AFL++ works (slides 8 and 9).
6.  AFLplusplus project. (n.d.). AFLplusplus [Source code and documentation]. https://github.com/AFLplusplus/AFLplusplus Used for: the commands, environment variables and crash file naming in the demo and exercise (slides 9, 11 and 12).
7.  Google. (n.d.). OSS-Fuzz: Continuous fuzzing for open source software. https://github.com/google/oss-fuzz Used for: the tools slide (slide 8).
8.  MacIver, D. R., et al. (n.d.). Hypothesis documentation. https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io Used for: the Hypothesis row on the tools slide (slide 8).
